## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No — emphatically not.**

**LLM assessment:** This clears both altitudes of the category-reflex test. It is neither the emerald-and-gold arabesque devotional cliché nor the cream-sage-serif wellness cliché — the two reflexes PRODUCT.md explicitly names and rejects, and the design genuinely avoids both. The "dark room, glowing word" thesis is committed in code: a near-black green-undertoned surface, Amiri as the hero graphic, achromatic luminance glow (never a colored bloom, never gold — not one gold token in the system). Motion is disciplined and load-bearing (the rise-into-light flourish fires once on the hero; `forwards` not `both` so a backgrounded tab never strands scripture invisible; the 286-verse blur-trap is deliberately neutralized on the reading surface). This is craft, not generation.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` over `web/index.html` + `web/src` returned exit 0 — one `single-font` warning, which is a **false positive**: it parsed only static `index.html` (visibly naming Amiri) and missed that Inter loads on the same line as the Latin UI font. The Amiri (Arabic hero) + Inter (Latin UI) pairing is exactly correct — two scripts, two jobs, per the register. No absolute-ban violations: no side-stripes, no gradient text, no over-round, no ghost-card border+shadow pairing, no eyebrow/numbered-marker scaffolding.

**Visual overlays:** Not available — the Chrome window was minimized, and Interceptor has no programmatic un-minimize. Visual assessment ran on the accessibility tree, extracted text, and the full CSS instead (direct, not visual). Screenshot evidence is the one gap in this run.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-made interface with a clear soul — the rarest thing in this category. The design system is disciplined, the accessibility intent is real (not bolted on), and the honesty doctrine (dual translation always, honest silence, attribution inline) is expressed *in the layout*, not just the copy. The single biggest opportunity isn't aesthetic — it's a **confirmed routing bug that silently breaks the app's own share/bookmark/reload story for every returning user.** Fix that and the app's headline feature (share a verse) actually works end-to-end.

## What's Working

1. **Attribution as design, not fine print.** Every rendering names its source inline in the reading surface — the lead chip, the "oleh [name]" byline, the four-scholar source stack where none is ranked above another. This is Principle 3 rendered structurally, and it's the thing no generic Qur'an app does.
2. **The crisis surface is designed against its own grain — correctly.** It deliberately does NOT reuse the amber `.caution` style (that means "this translation is contested"). It's the calmest, most solid panel in the app: full border, no alarm color, no shouting icon at someone already frightened. Ranking a human life above a rendering dispute was consciously avoided. Rare judgment.
3. **Honesty is structural.** The boot-error banner tells the truth ("reading still works — the index is inlined, not fetched"), honest silence has a real surface, the min-score floor stops confident junk, and the explainer even argues *against* the product ("telling the truth against ourselves"). The whole system refuses to fabricate.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Deep-link routing is clobbered by thread restore — share/bookmark/reload broken for returning users
- **Why it matters:** On cold load, `route()` correctly renders a deep-linked `#/surah/N`, `#/tema/X`, or `#/baca` — then `bootCorpus().then(restoreThread)` fires, and `restoreThread()` calls `showChat()` unconditionally (`main.ts:613`), stomping the route. Any user with a saved conversation (i.e. every returning user) who opens a shared verse link, reloads on the reading surface, or bookmarks a surah gets **snapped back to chat**. The app generates these links itself ("Baca lanjutan Al-Kahfi →" → `#/surah/18#10`) and ships a Share button built entirely on them. Confirmed live (cold-loaded `#/surah/1` → chat) and in code.
- **Fix:** Guard the restore's visibility flip. `restoreThread()` should populate the thread DOM without forcing `showChat()` when `location.hash` is a non-root route — let `route()` own visibility. One conditional.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] English theme labels on /tema — the product's own named anti-reference, shipped
- **Why it matters:** The theme browser renders "Hardship & ease", "Anxiety & fear", "Grief & loss", "Provision & debt", "Trust in God" — English category names to an Indonesian-only audience, with franken-slugs (`hardship-dan-ease`: English words joined by Indonesian *dan*). "English leaking into an Indonesian product" is a wound PRODUCT.md explicitly calls out, and here it's on a primary browse surface. A Gen-Z user arriving at 2am with grief sees an English word for their grief. Source: `theme-index.ts:23,64,105,222,263`.
- **Fix:** Translate the 12 theme labels to Indonesian (Kesulitan & kelapangan, Cemas & takut, Duka & kehilangan, Rezeki & utang, Tawakal…) and regenerate slugs. The English keys can stay internal.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Crisis banner is phone-only, on a line documented as slow to answer
- **Why it matters:** `crisis.ts` offers only "Telepon 119 → tekan 8." The number is real (SEJIWA/Kemenkes, verified), but the public line is reported to answer slowly due to limited staff — and the person reading this is at 2am, in crisis. Two faster official channels exist and aren't offered: WhatsApp **0811-3855-472** and **healing119.id** (chat/voice, no registration). For the founding persona (Rifqi, 19, in debt, 2am), a slow phone line is a real gap.
- **Fix:** Add the WhatsApp and web channels alongside the phone CTA. Keep the calm, steady visual treatment. Erik to eyeball final copy before ship.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P2] No "last read" bookmark — the come-back-to-the-verse-that-held-you gap
- **Why it matters:** A reader who found a verse at 2am has no way back to it next time; reload even actively dumps them out of the reading surface (see P1 routing). PRODUCT.md's emotional arc is "relief, then curiosity" — returning to the verse that gave relief is the natural second visit, and there's no surface for it. No gamification needed; a single quiet "lanjutkan bacaan" entry point.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** Shares a verse from the app → the recipient (any prior user) taps the link and lands on the chat empty state, not the verse — the share is silently broken. Reloads mid-read after an interruption → back to chat, place lost. Both are the routing bug; both hit exactly Casey's interruption-and-return behavior. *(Mitigations present: composer is bottom/thumb-zone, 44px targets, state persistence exists — the bug is that persistence fights routing.)*

**Jordan (First-Timer):** Well served on the whole — the proactive "why two translations?" explainer is offered *before* the concept confuses, seeds show real language, nav is word-labeled. One snag: on /tema the first thing Jordan reads is English category names, which quietly signals "this wasn't built for me" — the exact feeling the product exists to remove.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** Strong intent — aria-labels on icon buttons, live region for status, RTL/lang on Arabic, WCAG-AA token discipline, reduced-motion everywhere, attribution in the accessible name. Not screen-reader-verified this run (visual/AT audit blocked by the minimized window), but the code shows genuine care. Worth a real NVDA/VoiceOver pass to confirm the source stack reads "who said what" in order.

## Minor Observations

- Header on phones carries info + display-trigger + 3 nav links; mitigated by collapsing the display controls behind one trigger below the tablet breakpoint — good instinct, worth a real-device thumb check.
- Icon-only header buttons (info, theme, display) are aria-labeled and titled but visually iconic; fine for the register, but the info "ⓘ" is the one a first-timer most needs to find.
- The `landed` highlight on an arrived-at verse is a nice touch that survives reduced-motion as a static border — correct.

## Questions to Consider

- The Share button is a headline feature and the routing bug makes it silently fail for returning users. Is share worth shipping visibly before deep-links resolve correctly?
- Should reload/deep-link *into a specific verse* also become the "last read" mechanism — one fix serving two gaps?
- The theme labels are English because they're derived from `problem-verses.ts` internal keys. Is there anywhere else the internal English vocabulary surfaces to users?
